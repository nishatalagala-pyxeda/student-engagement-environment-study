# Broad environmental mood model

This is an exploratory binary model of lower (1–2) versus higher (3–4) reported PAM valence. It does not model emotional engagement or establish environmental effects on mood.

The initial model uses outdoor weather plus median indoor temperature/humidity across a fixed eight-room panel. These indoor measures describe campus context, not the student's classroom. Later expanded comparisons below also include campus CO₂/noise proxies; their room mapping remains unknown.

## Run

Use the project environment:

```bash
.venv/bin/python broad_environment_model.py
```

The script accepts `--data-dir`, `--survey-zip` and `--output-dir`. Defaults use the two Downloads datasets verified in this conversation. All output is written under `outputs/broad-environment-model`; original data is read only. A rerun replaces generated outputs.

Dependencies: numpy, pandas, scikit-learn, matplotlib and joblib. The exact installed versions used are saved in metrics.json, and requirements-model.txt records direct modeling package versions. The local environment uses the bundled runtime's numpy/pandas without modifying that runtime.

## Evaluation

- Primary: leave one complete survey date out; nine dates total.
- Chronological check: train on October 28–November 1 and test on November 18–22, 2019.
- Supplemental: hold out students; shared dates make this a different test from future-context generalization.
- Majority and time-of-day baselines, outdoor/indoor ablations, logistic regression and a shallow random forest.
- Three conflicting participant/timestamp pairs (six labels) excluded from the main dataset, with a sensitivity check retaining all 169 labels.

Feature preparation uses six five-minute readings ending at/before submission. All scaling and imputation are fitted only on training folds. No participant identifier or survey-derived predictor enters a model. Hyperparameters are fixed; the primary model is prespecified environmental logistic regression, not whichever comparator scores best.

Read `outputs/broad-environment-model/report.html` before interpreting predictions. The exported joblib payload expects the already prepared features listed in its `features` field and stores the fitted preprocessing pipeline and target definition.

## Valence regression

Run `.venv/bin/python valence_regression.py` to predict the original 1–4 valence scores. It reuses the verified prepared inputs and compares mean/median baselines, time-only ridge, environmental ridge and a shallow random forest, including outdoor/indoor ablations. ## Stress-history versus environmental hypothesis

Run `stress_environment_hypothesis.py`, followed by `stress_association_sensitivity.py`, using the temporary or restored runtime described above. Outputs are separate in `outputs/stress-environment-hypothesis/`. The saved analysis definitions distinguish incremental prediction from within-person association; no causal effect is identified. All original source data and earlier analyses remain unchanged.

Historical stress uses all valid PSS-only cleaned reports, each at least seven days before its target. Test-block histories are frozen before the training cutoff. This differs from the earlier baseline using only environmentally matched training reports. Compare identical history/control models with and without the same 14 environmental features, on the previously inspected April test and on expanding, purged 14-day blocks. The latter score 165 responses from 25 IDs across Fall and Spring. They are exploratory follow-ups, not an untouched external validation.

Adding environment worsens rolling-test MAE from 0.427 to 0.462 for ridge and from 0.457 to 0.470 for forest. Past-average stress alone scores 0.409 versus the training-wide mean's 0.608. Environment does not show additional predictive value in these comparisons.

Within-person/calendar-week fixed-effect associations use 197 nonoverlapping responses from 30 IDs, with and without earlier stress adjustment. Seven environmental means enter jointly, with VOC/PM/light log transformed. Person-cluster CR1 covariance and restricted-null wild-cluster inference are used, with individual-test BH correction. Full OLS coefficients and covariance independently reproduce the residualized calculation. The joint history-adjusted environmental test has p=0.009; without history adjustment p=0.0595. No individual exposure passes seven-test correction. Leaving one ID out preserves the positive VOC estimate, but a first-difference check (156 changes, 30 IDs; exact differences of week indicators and history controls) does not confirm the joint association, p=0.377. These post-result diagnostics are explicitly exploratory.

The evidence therefore supports stress-history prediction, but neither proves an environmental causal effect nor proves its absence. Confounding, reverse causation, exposure error, short-panel lag bias and persistent environmental pathways remain. The report includes the mixed results rather than equating failed prediction with no causal effect.

## Does environment matter more when prior stress is higher?

Run `stress_environment_moderation.py` with the same runtime. `stress_moderation_features.py` supplies reusable train-only transformations. Outputs are separate in `outputs/stress-environment-moderation/`. The primary moderator is continuous latest pre-exposure PSS, at least seven days earlier; a sensitivity uses the first valid score before each student's first matched exposure week. Person/week fixed-effect interaction tests include all main effects and a quadratic prior-stress term. Seven interaction terms are tested jointly with restricted-null wild person-cluster inference, with individual BH correction. A first-difference sensitivity uses differences of interaction products, not products of differences.

The weekly-prior interaction test uses 197 responses from 30 IDs and gives p=0.592. First-baseline and first-difference sensitivities give p=0.280 and p=0.328. No individual interaction passes correction. These tests do not support stronger environmental relationships at higher prior stress, but wide intervals do not establish equal responses.

Nested ridge predictors compare history/control, additive environmental terms, and seven explicit stress/environment products. Environmental transformations, imputation, centering/scaling and product construction use training data only. Of 165 rolling-test responses, 154 have observed prior stress; their MAEs are 0.404, 0.436 and 0.440 respectively. The remaining 11 unknown-history responses are reported separately and cannot answer moderation. The higher-prior group (score≥2, a descriptive midpoint rather than a clinical label) has 36 responses from 13 IDs; the lower group has 118 responses from 20 IDs. Adding interactions over additive environment has no reliable improvement or higher-versus-lower differential benefit. April has only three higher-prior responses, so subgroup inference is suppressed there.

Checks independently verify history timing, train-only transformations even under extreme test inputs, interaction sign/zero behavior at prior scores 1/2/3, conditional slope contrasts, full-OLS/FWL coefficient/covariance agreement and serialization. This remains exploratory predictive/association moderation, not causal effect modification.
